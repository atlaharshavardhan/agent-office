## Background (re-confirmed briefly)
- Nov 8, 2026: NVIDIA's board announced an independent investigation after reports that Huang was the target of a foreign-intelligence effort. The board said it had "no reason to believe" national security or NVIDIA secrets were compromised. The same day, Huang admitted an affair in an internal message, without naming the woman (Axios, CNBC, CBS, NBC, Nov 8).
- Nov 12, 2026: Huang resigned as CEO and from the board, effective immediately. Colette Kress became interim CEO, and Harvey C. Jones became chairman (NVIDIA release; NBC; TechCrunch timeline; Forbes Nov 16).
- The board hired Wachtell, Lipton for the inquiry (Yahoo Finance, citing the Reuters report).

## Safe narration rewrite (~100 words)

> The most powerful man in AI chips just walked away, and the story runs through Singapore.
> Jensen Huang resigned as NVIDIA's CEO on November 12th. At the center of the reporting is Alice Huang, no relation, the former CEO of Megaspeed, a Singapore-based NVIDIA customer.
> US Commerce and Justice officials say they've been investigating whether Megaspeed's chips illegally reached China. No charges have been filed.
> The New York Times reported that US officials suspect she worked for Chinese intelligence, and that the two had a relationship. Reuters reports Jensen called it routine business to NVIDIA executives.
> China calls the spy claims groundless. Megaspeed denies wrongdoing. Nothing has been proven.

(About 105 words. Every allegation is tied to a named outlet or official, and the ending states that nothing is proven.)

## Legal/platform cautions
- **Defamation risk is with Alice Huang and Megaspeed.** Calling her a "Chinese spy", or saying chips "were smuggled" or "reached China", as **fact** is the main exposure. Always attribute: "US officials reportedly suspect…", "The New York Times reported…". Add the denial and "not proven / no charges" in the same breath. The DOJ, Singapore and the CIA (as reported) have not concluded she is a spy.
- **Private individual.** She holds no public office and has made no public statements. Even if a court treated her as a limited-purpose public figure, a private-individual standard means a lower bar for liability, so be conservative:
  - avoid speculating about her personal life beyond what reputable outlets reported;
  - no "honey trap", "seductress" or similar framing in the title, thumbnail or voiceover;
  - don't show private or intimate photos beyond what is necessary; the Reuters photos are copyrighted, so license them or don't use them;
  - don't speculate about where she is now.
- **Jensen Huang** is a public figure, and he has publicly admitted *an* affair. "Reportedly" is still needed when linking that affair to Alice Huang, because he did not name her. CBS reports that its own sources confirmed she was the woman.
- **Thumbnail and title count.** YouTube and courts read the whole package. A hedged voiceover does not cure a headline like "NVIDIA CEO's CHINESE SPY LOVER". Use "alleged" or "suspected" and attribute in on-screen text too.
- **YouTube policies.** Harassment/cyberbullying rules cover content targeting identifiable individuals, including suggestive framing. Misinformation and "sensitive events" labels may apply, so add sources in the description. Don't use sexualized imagery of a real person.
- **Numbers.** Do not quote dollar figures ($2B, $4.8B, $12B) as established. They are conflicting reported estimates.
- **Fast-moving story.** Re-check before upload. Developments since Nov 12 include a congressional testimony request, the DOJ/Commerce statement and Jensen's Nov 17 statement.
