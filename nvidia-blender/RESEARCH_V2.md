## Safe narration wording

1. "In early October, Nvidia hit a record of about $5.8 trillion, the most valuable company ever." (Or "about $5.77 trillion on October 6.") Do not say $6 trillion.
2. "By Monday's close it was worth about $3.8 trillion." (Or "$3.76 trillion".) If the video runs after tonight's earnings, add "as of November 16".
3. "That's about $2 trillion gone, roughly 35%, in about six weeks."
4. "That loss alone is bigger than all of Meta, a company worth about $1.6 trillion."
5. "Nvidia sells an estimated 8 in 10 AI chips." Keep the hedge ("an estimated" or "analysts estimate"). Better still: "about 80% of AI chips, by most estimates."
6. "Big Tech plans to spend over $700 billion on AI infrastructure this year." (Or "on capex".)
7. "On Election Day, voters backed limits on new data centers in 40 of 41 local ballot measures, and chip stocks tanked days later." Present it as one worry, not the sole cause, e.g. "one of several fears hitting the stock."
8. "Investors also worry about 'circular' deals: Bank of America estimates Nvidia has around $70 billion invested in AI companies and up to $230 billion in financial guarantees, some backing its own customers."
9. "Then, on November 12, Jensen Huang, Nvidia's co-founder and CEO of more than 30 years, resigned, days after the board opened an investigation. CFO Colette Kress is now interim CEO." Do NOT state the spy or affair allegations as fact. If you mention them: "following reports that he was targeted by a foreign intelligence effort." The board said it had no reason to believe company secrets were compromised.
10. "Its last quarter brought in $96.2 billion in revenue, up 106% from a year earlier, and new results land November 17." (Or "today/tonight", depending on the publish date.)
11. "On November 5 alone, it fell 9.26%, wiping out about $450 billion in a single day."

## Recommended replacements / cautions
- Nothing is fully UNVERIFIED. The softer points are:
  - Claim 1: the exact record close price conflicts between sources. Use the market cap ("about $5.8T") and the date, not a share price.
  - Claim 4: use "about $1.6 trillion" for Meta, not "$1.59T". I found no exact Nov 16 close figure.
  - Claim 5: this is an estimate (range 70–88%). Always hedge.
  - Claim 7: don't call data-center votes the cause of the decline. Make it "one of several pressures", alongside capex/AI-bubble fears, circular financing, the CEO exit and new US chip-export rules (Nov 16).
  - Claim 8: attribute $230B to Bank of America. NVIDIA's own 10-Q shows ~$108.5B in guarantees.
- Timing risk: NVIDIA reports after the close on Nov 17, and the stock and market cap may move sharply. Pin the figures to "as of Nov 16 close" on screen.
- Claim 9 date nuance: the announcement was Nov 12 (the 8-K's filename suggests board action Nov 11). "On November 12, Nvidia announced…" is the safest phrasing.
